Why two ROE figures

The parent company's return on equity is 16.0%; the group's is 14.9%. A data vendor quoting 14.9% would look like a disagreement. Checked by the contract, it is BASIS_MISMATCH (reference CONSOLIDATED vs pilot STANDALONE): both numbers are right, on different bases. A system that prints "ROE 16.0%" without the basis has made a claim the reader cannot check.

How each cell was decided

  • Basis is printed from the basis the figure is actually on, never the one that was requested. The guided growth figure was requested on a group basis, but management did not say which basis it meant, so the page says exactly that.
  • Status and confidence follow the source: computed from filed statements is company reported, high; stated in a presentation is company disclosed, medium.
  • Derived figures inherit their inputs' basis and the weakest input's confidence. The P/E divides a market price by group EPS, so it is on a group basis. Group EPS divided by parent book value would be refused rather than printed.
  • Refusals are named. Had any figure lacked a lawful basis, it would appear here as a named refusal code instead of a number. None did on this page.
